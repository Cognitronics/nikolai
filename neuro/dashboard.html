03 — Live

NikolAI lives in a small world of five places — hut, orchard, forest, river and workshop — in real time, with its current brain. Everything it hears comes from its teacher; everything it says, it has learned by itself.

Talk to it

Ask it something – “Where are you?”, “How are you?”, “What do you have?”, “What do you want?”, “What did you do?” – or say anything. It answers with what it learned from reading and from its teacher. Give one of the teacher’s instructions and you are the teacher: if it follows, it learns.

04 — Reading

It reads stories.
And learns from them.

NikolAI reads what a child hears and reads by about twelve – some 100 million words, all from people: parents talking to toddlers, conversations, children’s books, film subtitles, Simple Wikipedia (the BabyLM corpus). It keeps guessing the next word – nobody tells it what words mean. That reading brain is its hearing and speaking. The question: does it then understand instructions with words its teacher never used?

Words it only knows from books

From a start that doesn’t give the answer away (several things to choose from, or the hut with four ways), 4 steps: with the sentence, without it. The teacher never says these words. Control: the same sentence with a made-up word (“Blick the stone!”) – if that works just as well, it only reacted to the noun.

06 — Imagination

How it pictures things.
Sharp where it knows, fog where it doesn’t.

NikolAI has never seen a picture. Every thing in its world has real properties – colour, size, shape, surface, parts – and it only learns them by seeing, taking, carrying and eating. From the heard word alone, its brain predicts what to expect and how sure it is. Each picture overlays several guesses drawn from that expectation: where it is sure, they agree and the picture is sharp; where things vary (apples: red, green, yellow), the guesses differ; where it has no experience, there is only fog.

07 — Tests

The evidence.
Raw numbers.

Understanding instructions

Success within 15 steps, 30 trials each — with the sentence, without it (same situation), and acting at random. Marked rows: never trained, only tested.

First test is running …

Combining words: big and small

A big and a small one lie on the floor (or it carries both), with other things around, 10 steps, 30 trials. Each thing is asked for both ways – “big …” and “small …” – from the same starts. When it takes (or puts down) that thing: how often the right size? If it ignores the size word, it gets one of the two right at most. “Understood”: both at least 70 %. “Noun only” = which size it takes for “Take a stone!”. Marked rows: the teacher never says “big stone” – NikolAI heard “big” and “small” with apples and branches, but “stone” only as “small stone”.
